# D4 — Transistor-level parameter database (SKY130)

**Files.**
* `data/sky130/parameter_database.csv`: 351 rows with the columns required by the brief (§6.2): Parameter, Symbol, Unit, Value, Physical meaning, Source, Source location, Operating conditions, Data classification, Uncertainty, Used in which equation. It is regenerated by `tools/build_parameter_db.py` from the pinned PDK files and this project's result files, and nothing in it is typed by hand except the class-I documentation rows. A rendered copy is in `results/tables/parameter_database.md`.
* `results/spice/device_params.csv`: 204 extracted parameter sets (4 device flavours × 4 geometries × 13 corner/temperature/supply conditions), produced by `sim/spice/device_char.py`.
* `results/spice/vdd_sweep_tt_*C.csv`: FO4 delay, switching energy and leakage versus supply.

This page summarises the database and explains how to read it. The values in the tables below are generated (`tools/make_report_tables.py`) from those files.

## 2. Extracted device parameters (class F: simulated from foundry cards)

Method (`sim/spice/device_char.py`): W/L = 1/0.15 µm single finger, source and bulk grounded (n) or at $V_{DD}$ (p), GMIN = 10⁻¹⁸ S. $V_{th,cc}$: gate voltage at $|I_D| = 100\,\text{nA}\cdot W/L$ for $|V_{DS}|$ = 50 mV (lin) and $= V_{DD}$ (sat); SS: minimum of $dV_G/d\log_{10}I_D$ in subthreshold at $|V_{DS}|=V_{DD}$; DIBL $=(V_{th,lin}-V_{th,sat})/(V_{DD}-0.05)$; $I_{on}$ at $|V_{GS}|=|V_{DS}|=V_{DD}$; $I_{off}$ at $V_{GS}=0$, $|V_{DS}|=V_{DD}$ (includes junction leakage); $C_{gg}$ by quasi-static gate ramp at $V_{DS}=0$. Vth depends on the extraction method (Ortiz-Conde et al. 2002). The constant-current method at 27 °C reproduces the foundry's documented model Vth at 7/0.15 µm within 5 mV (nfet tt/ff/ss: 0.648/0.617/0.682 V vs documented 0.645/0.615/0.677 V) and at 7/8 µm pfet_hvt within 3 mV. Other documented rows differ by 20–50 mV, and Idsat at 7/0.15 µm by +5–7 %. The documentation does not define fingering, bias or temperature, so this residual cannot be resolved.

**Statistical variation.** Mismatch enters as independent Gaussian terms per device (`AGAUSS`) on toxe, vth0, voff and nfactor, scaled by $1/\sqrt{W L \cdot mult}$. Example: inv_1 nfet (0.65 × 0.15 µm²), σ(Vth0) = 3.356/√0.0975 = 10.7 mV; pfet_hvt (1.0 × 0.15 µm²), σ = 14.2 mV **[derived]**. Global process variation is represented by corners (tt/ss/ff/sf/fs and the ll/hh/hl/lh variants) and by the `mc` section (Gaussian `AGAUSS` on named process parameters in `parameters/critical.spice`). The cards do not document correlation between parameters beyond what the corner definitions imply. A Gaussian distribution is the cards' own assumption, not this project's.

**Reading the sweep.**
* **Energy per transition** falls from 18.6 fJ at 1.8 V to 3.0 fJ at 0.8 V and 0.42 fJ at 0.3 V (25 °C). That is slightly faster than V², because the effective switched capacitance $E/V^2$ falls from 5.7 fF to 4.6 fF as the devices leave strong inversion.
* **Delay** grows from 84 ps (1.8 V) to 3.8 ns (0.8 V), 84 ns (0.6 V) and 71 µs (0.3 V). It grows 5–7× per 100 mV between 0.7 and 0.5 V and about one decade per 100 mV below 0.5 V, consistent with the subthreshold slope of the devices (§2).
* The **FO4 energy–delay product is lowest at 1.6 V** (25 °C) and 1.4 V (100 °C), within 2–6 % of its value at the 1.8 V nominal supply. For speed-constrained logic the nominal supply is close to optimal in this sense.
* **Temperature inversion.** At 1.95 and 1.8 V the 100 °C chain is 3 % and 1 % slower than at 25 °C. At 1.6 V and below it is faster: 1.5× at 1.0 V, 6.2× at 0.6 V, 30× at 0.3 V, because the threshold voltage falls with temperature faster than mobility does. The "slow, hot" sign-off corner is therefore not the slowest corner below ≈1.6 V. Low-voltage timing must be signed off cold.
* Inverter leakage rises ≈100× from 25 °C to 100 °C at 1.8 V (1.2 → 124 pW).

**Minimum-energy point of a MAC (class E model, D6 §8).** The MAC's energy at 1.8 V is expressed as an equivalent number of FO4 transitions and scaled with the FO4 energy curve. Its Liberty leakage at 1.8 V is scaled with the inverter leakage curve, and its cycle time with the FO4 delay. This assumes glitch fraction, logic depth in FO4 and leakage mix are independent of supply. All three are approximations, flagged in D12.

**Reading the model.**
* At 100 °C the modelled minimum-energy supply is 0.5 V: 0.45–0.47 pJ per MAC, 11–13× below 1.8 V, at cycle times of 2.6–7.5 µs.
* At 25 °C the high-Vt PMOS keeps leakage so low that the optimum moves to 0.3–0.35 V. That is at or below the lowest simulated point for the carry-save MAC, so it is reported as "≤ 0.3 V". The modelled energy is ≈0.17 pJ (30–33× below 1.8 V), but at millisecond cycle times.
* These are **class E indications, not design points**. The model scales one inverter. It ignores variability: a 3σ threshold shift of ≈40 mV against a ≈90 mV/decade slope changes a gate's delay by ≈3×. It also ignores flip-flop and hold-time behaviour at low supply, noise margins of stacked gates, and the change of glitch fraction with supply.
* The defensible conclusion is narrower: in SKY130 HD, an order of magnitude of energy per MAC is available from supply scaling alone, at a cost of 10³–10⁶× in throughput per unit area. Quantifying it for a real MAC needs the low-voltage characterisation of D14 Stage B.

**Interpretation.**
1. At 25 °C, Liberty and the tt model card agree within a factor of a few (geometric mean 0.73). For example, inv_1 with input low: Liberty 0.000196 nW, SPICE 0.000198 nW.
2. At 100 °C, the Liberty tables are **16× (tt), ≈560× (ss) and ≈4× (ff) above the same-named model corner**. They lie within a factor of ≈0.2–2 of the PDK's separate `leak` model set (`models/corners/leak.spice`), which no section of `sky130.lib.spice` references. This explains the observation in the input research file that `ss_100C_1v60` Liberty leakage exceeds `tt_100C_1v80`, "the opposite of the usual expectation". The hot-corner Liberty leakage appears to be characterised with a leakage-pessimistic model set. This is an inference: the characterisation conditions are undocumented.
3. Consequence: leakage at 100 °C carries an **uncertainty of one to three decades** depending on which foundry data set is taken as truth. The framework therefore carries leakage as an interval (Liberty as upper bound, tt model as lower bound) in the thermal calculations (D9). For the MAC arrays in this project, leakage at the upper bound (Liberty ff, 100 °C) is 0.7 % (carry-save PE) to 1.3 % (ripple PE) of the dynamic power at the sign-off clock, so the conclusions do not depend on it (D9 §5).
4. At −40 °C the DC currents of stacked cells fall below the resolution of the operating-point analysis (|I| ≲ 1 fA; some states return negative values). They are excluded and irrelevant for energy.
